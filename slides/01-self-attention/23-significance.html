Significance, caveats, and the actual contribution

This was a translation paper. Two MT tasks, one parsing task, 65M and 213M parameters, sentences around a hundred tokens long. Everything it is now famous for was established by later work.

That is not a criticism. It is the difference between what a paper proved and what a field built on it - and keeping those apart is most of what a journal club is for.

The architecture split within a year: strip the decoder and you get BERT, strip the encoder and you get GPT. Both are this paper's Figure 1 with one half deleted.
Step 1 - What the architecture turned into.
2017 · Transformer encoder–decoder, built for translation
↙↘
encoder only Bidirectional, sees the whole input at once. Good at understanding a text you already have. BERT (2018) → RoBERTa, embeddings, retrieval
decoder only Masked self-attention, no cross-attention - nothing to attend to. Good at continuing a text. GPT (2018) → GPT-3/4, Claude, Llama…

What this paper did not show

01Scaling. Nothing here says the architecture keeps improving at 100× the size. Scaling laws are 2020.
02Language modelling or in-context learning. Not tested. Not mentioned.
03Long context. O(n²·d) is only cheap because n < d here. §4 says so explicitly. The next decade was spent fighting that.
04Interpretability. §4 hedges - heads "could yield" more interpretable models, shown with appendix examples, never measured.
05Durability of the details. Post-LN → pre-LN. Sinusoids → RoPE. ReLU → SwiGLU. Multi-head → grouped-query. The skeleton survived; almost every organ was replaced.
THE TAKEAWAY

Not: "this paper invented attention" - Bahdanau did, in 2014, and by 2017 it was standard equipment.

It showed that attention could be the entire sequence-processing mechanism - that once you have it, the recurrence underneath is not doing work worth its cost. The title is a claim about sufficiency, and that claim turned out to hold far beyond translation.
