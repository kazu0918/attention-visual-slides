01 TRANSLATION AI

AI That Translates —
The Encoder–Decoder Model

Encoder–Decoder Model

Because word order differs across languages, translation isn't just word-by-word. Instead, the model first "digests" the whole sentence into a single meaning, then builds the output from that.

The input sentence is turned entirely into numbers (a vector) that capture its meaning, and the output words are generated from that vector.

Note: this is a simplified picture of how a translation model works — it's not necessarily how translation happens in the human brain.

02 WORDS AS NUMBERS

Turning Meaning
Into Numbers

word2vec

The core idea behind word2vec is to represent a word's meaning as a list of numbers — a vector. More numbers (dimensions) can capture finer shades of meaning.

Words with similar meanings end up close together in this numeric space.

03 ATTENTION

Meaning Depends
on Context

Attention

"Apple" can mean a fruit or a company. A word's meaning isn't fixed by itself — it's shaped by the words around it.

Attention updates a word's vector using the vectors of the surrounding context words. Here, that's shown as Apple's point shifting toward the fruit or tech cluster depending on the sentence.

Simplification: a real Transformer doesn't "look up a map" like this — it blends the actual words in that one sentence. But real contextual embeddings do show the same effect: the same word lands in noticeably different places depending on meaning.

05 SELF-ATTENTION

Comparing Every Word
With Every Other Word

Dot-Product Similarity

Self-attention's basic idea, put simply: for every vector, compute how close in meaning it is to every other vector in the sentence, using their dot product — brute-force, all pairs.

Take the 2D vectors from the last slide, multiply their matching components and add them up, and repeat for every pair to build a similarity matrix.

In a real Transformer this is computed as Q·Kᵀ (query vectors against key vectors), but the intuition is the same: "how relevant is token j to token i?"

06 SOFTMAX

From Scores to
Blend Ratios

Softmax Normalization

Apply softmax to each row of the matrix. That makes every row sum to 1 — turning raw similarity scores into a proportion: how much of each other word to blend in.

For company-context Apple, most of the weight lands on "laptop." For fruit-context apple, most of it lands on "juicy."

Softmax exaggerates differences — a slightly higher score gets a disproportionately larger share of the weight.
Blend weights for apple's row — every bar adds up to 100%.
07 BLENDING

Blending the
Vectors

Weighted Sum

Now actually use those blend ratios: multiply each token's vector by its weight and add them all together. The result is apple's new, context-aware vector.

08 ALL TOKENS

Every Word
Gets Updated

Contextualized Embeddings

Self-attention repeats this same process for every token in the sentence, not just "apple" — each one gets its own similarity-based blend.

The word starts at the same point in both sentences, but ends up in very different places — one pulled toward "fruit," the other toward "tech."

This is what gives Transformer-based models contextual understanding: the same token can carry different meanings depending on its sentence.
